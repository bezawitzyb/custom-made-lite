Design options in the drawer you already open

Three design options added inside the "Select size and fit" drawer, without slowing down the customer who changes nothing. Any changed detail makes the pair Custom Made: 1-2 weeks instead of 1-2 days.

Why a single pleat: it is the only option with demand data. Lyst searches for pleated trousers rose 110% (FashionUnited, June 2026). It adds room through the thigh and seat without a bigger waist, and Suitsupply already makes it on Slim and Wide, so it is a known make.

Why this placement

Everything lives in the drawer "Select size and fit" already opens, so the product page is unchanged. Today's yes/no fit question becomes "How do you want to choose?".

The trade-off, made explicit

One extra screen (Details) for people who change something; none for people who don't. The check sits on the size screen, so there is no summary screen.

Built for limited product knowledge

Every option is a photo with one line on what it does ("small buckles on each hip, adjust the waist without a belt"), and the standard choice is preselected, so Next changes nothing.

Taps from "Select size and fit" to Add to bag. No study ties a click count to conversion (Nielsen Norman Group); the A/B test checks whether picking's extra taps cost orders.

Why the stylist waits: Help me choose is built in the prototype but ships later as its own A/B test. A small first release tests the options on their own; the stylist is then judged against version 1.

What I deliberately left out

  • A free-text AI chat. Costly, hard to keep on-brand and to translate for every EU market. Fixed rules give the suggestion and the reason.
  • More values per option (5 cm turn-up, double pleat). Two clear choices per option; people stall when choices are hard to tell apart.
  • Fabric, pockets, lining. That is the full Custom Made configurator, not Lite.
  • A surcharge. The price stays at €179 so the test measures the options, not a price change.
  • Photo or body-scan sizing. The strongest next step for returns, but a big build with privacy risk. Find my size is the cheap version.

What I'd validate before building it for real

  • Usability test with 8-10 shoppers. Do they find the options and understand a side adjuster from the photo alone?
  • Production: can every mix be made, and does the 1-2 week Custom Made lead time hold at Lite's volume?
  • Legal and Customer Service: do these pairs follow the Custom Made return rule (30 days, unworn and unaltered) in every market?
  • Data: today's panel opens, fit-step skip rate, order and return rates, so the thresholds and test length are real, not my guesses.

What I'm least sure about

  • Whether people will wait 1-2 weeks. Any change makes a pair Custom Made. The flow states the wait wherever it applies, and the test watches cancellations before shipping.
  • Demand for side adjusters and turn-ups. I found no data for either; only the pleat has numbers behind it.
  • Returns read late. A Custom Made pair takes 1-2 weeks to arrive plus 30 days to return, so the cost of the options shows 5-6 weeks after an order. Orders decide at week 8, returns at week 15.
  • Whether a stylist beats picture cards. No evidence either way for custom clothing, which is why it is a separate phase 2 test.
