Junior PM case · Custom Made Lite · My reasoning

Choose your trouser details, in the same few taps

Customers can now change three details of their trousers. The new choices sit in the panel that opens when you tap "Select size and fit". If you change nothing, buying is as quick as today. If you change something, the pair is made for you, which I assume takes 1-2 weeks instead of 1-2 days.

Why I picked the pleat:

  • More people want it. Demand for pleated trousers on Lyst has more than doubled since 2025, up 110% (FashionUnited, June 2026). An earlier sign: searches for men's pleated trousers were up 97% on the year before (Trendalytics, 2023).
  • It makes trousers more comfortable. A pleat opens up when you sit, giving room at the hips, seat and thighs (Art of Manliness), and hides extra room in the lap without a bigger waist (Proper Cloth).
  • Other made-to-order brands offer it. Indochino, Hockerty and Mond all let customers choose pleats.
  • Suitsupply already makes it. Slim and Wide come with a pleat today, and the relaxed range has pleats too (Suitsupply), so it is easy to produce.

No source gives pleat data for the Netherlands or for Suitsupply customers, so the test will show real demand.

Where the choices go, and why

The product page does not change. When you tap "Select size and fit", the first question is now "How do you want to choose?". You can keep the trousers as shown, or pick the details yourself.

The trade-off

People who change something see one extra screen. People who change nothing see no extra screens. Their choices are shown again on the size screen, so there is no separate check screen.

No tailoring words needed

Each choice is a photo with one short line, like "small buckles on each hip, so you don't need a belt". The usual choice is already selected, so tapping Next changes nothing.

Taps from "Select size and fit" to "Add to bag". No study shows that fewer clicks always means more sales (Nielsen Norman Group), so the test will show whether the extra taps lose orders.

Why "Help me choose" comes later: it asks three quick questions and suggests a pair. It is in the prototype, but I would launch it later as a separate test. That way the first launch stays small, and we learn what the new choices do on their own first.

What I deliberately left out

  • More than two choices per detail, like a double pleat. Too many similar choices make people hesitate.
  • Fabric, pockets and lining. That belongs in the full Custom Made service, not the quick version.
  • An extra charge. The price stays €179, so the test shows the effect of the choices, not of a price change.
  • Sizing from a photo or body scan. It could cut returns a lot, but it is a big build and raises privacy questions. "Find my size" is a simple first step.
  • An AI chat where people type. It costs a lot, can say the wrong thing, and needs translating for every country. Simple fixed rules do the job.

What I would check before building it

  • Does the new first question slow people down? Most people change nothing, and many skip today's fit step. I would time 8-10 shoppers buying the pair as shown, on today's flow and on mine. If mine is slower: keep "as shown" as the default and make "Change fit or details" a small link.
  • Do people understand the choices from the photos? Same session. Pass: 8 of 10 can say what a side adjuster and a turn-up do without help. If not: better photos and words, before we build anything.
  • Can every combination be made, and how fast? Ask Production. The 1-2 weeks on this page is my assumption. If it is longer: change the wait everywhere it is shown, and expect more cancellations.
  • What does a returned pair cost us? These pairs cannot go back into stock. Ask Finance for the cost of one return, and Legal and Customer Service whether the return rule (30 days, unworn, not altered) works in every country. This sets the return rate that would make us stop the test.
  • Today's real numbers. How many people open "Select size and fit", how many skip the fit step, and today's order and return rates. These replace my guesses and decide how long the test runs.

What I am least sure about

  • That the new first question costs nothing. It adds a choice for everyone, including people who only want their size. In the test, I would watch orders from people who change nothing. They must not drop.
  • That people will get the size right first time. A wrong size on a made-for-you pair cannot be resold. "Find my size" adds 2 cm of room at the waist, which is my own guess, and returns show late (1-2 weeks to arrive plus 30 days to return). So orders are judged at week 8, returns only at week 15.
  • That people will wait 1-2 weeks. That time is my own estimate. In the test, I would watch cancellations and how many people switch back to the standard pair when they see the wait.
  • That the photos will be good enough. The prototype uses edited images, and some colours are recoloured. Real photos of every combination in every colour are a real cost that I have not counted.
  • That people want side adjusters and turn-ups. I found no numbers for either. Only the pleat has data behind it. The test shows which choices people use, so a choice nobody picks can be removed.
